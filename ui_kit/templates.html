§ Plate 04 · Capstone · 2026
Where the watershed bends
⌖ 19.4326° S · 65.4536° W
Project
Pilcomayo Basin · heavy metals
Role
Co-author · field analyst
Advisor
Dr. Arturo Keller · UCSB
Status
Manuscript in preparation

The Pilcomayo descends 1,500 kilometres from the Andes into the Gran Chaco, gathering sediment, silver, and a long historical load of cadmium, lead, and zinc along the way. Our work tracks how those metals partition between dissolved phase, suspended sediment, and the river bed across seasons.

Sampling was conducted across thirty-two stations over fourteen months, paired with discharge gauges and a bench-top XRF for sediment cores. Geochemically targeted remediation strategies were modelled against three flow regimes.

Method

Spatiotemporal partitioning was modelled using a coupled hydrological-geochemical framework. Field data was processed in R; sediment cores were imaged and indexed in Python with a small ResNet for grain classification.

Findings

Cadmium concentrations in the lower transect exceeded baseline by 3.2σ during low-flow months. A staged remediation plan — wetland buffer reactivation paired with targeted sediment capping — reduced modelled exposure by 41%.
